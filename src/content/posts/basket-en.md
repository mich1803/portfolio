Ever since I was a child, I have always been passionate about basketball, a sport that still occupies an enormous place in my heart, as I continue to play it and, above all, to be moved by it.

That is why I want to treat this blog post almost like a personal diary: a way to tell and remember an experience I do not want to lose, because it truly means so much to me.

## June 26, 2026

[Luka Dončić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luka_Don%C4%8Di%C4%87) is in Rome to present the new team he owns <sup class="source-citation"><a href="https://www.ilmessaggero.it/video/roma/luka_doncic_roma_torneo_san_lorenzo-9613962.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="Source 1">[1]</a></sup>.

Ever since the NBA announced that it wanted to enter Europe and Italy <sup class="source-citation"><a href="https://sport.sky.it/nba/2025/11/07/nba-europe-2027-roma-milano-come-funziona" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="Source 2">[2]</a></sup>, with the idea of having one team in Milan and another in Rome, two groups immediately seized the opportunity to put themselves forward as the Capital's “new” team.

Rome, in fact, had been left without a team in the top division after the end of the historic Virtus Roma <sup class="source-citation"><a href="https://www.gazzetta.it/Basket/SerieA/09-12-2020/finale-triste-roma-si-ritira-serie-a-si-va-avanti-15-squadre-3901545176988.shtml?utm_source=chatgpt.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="Source 3">[3]</a></sup>.

Dončić, together with his former General Manager [Donnie Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Nelson), acquires Vanoli Cremona to turn it into **BC Roma SPQR** <sup class="source-citation"><a href="https://sport.sky.it/basket/2026/05/29/doncic-basket-squadra-roma" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="Source 4">[4]</a></sup>.

On the other side, Paul Matiasic, former president of Trieste, acquires Brescia to create **Maxima Roma** <sup class="source-citation"><a href="https://sport.sky.it/basket/serie-a/2026/06/26/germani-basket-brescia-maxima-roma-fip-news" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="Source 5">[5]</a></sup>

Two moves that were not welcomed by everyone, as they left two important groups of fans without their teams, in cities that have given a great deal to Italian basketball in recent years.

In the months that followed, a real battle would begin, strictly off the court, to win over as large an audience as possible.

And the funny thing is that, one way or another, [**Francesco Totti**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Totti) always ends up in the middle of it.

Totti joins the Maxima Roma project <sup class="source-citation"><a href="https://sport.sky.it/basket/serie-a/2026/09/01/francesco-totti-maxima-roma-basket" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="Source 6">[6]</a></sup>, while Dončić will be at the Stadio Olimpico to promote BC Roma SPQR during the early-season title clash between Roma and Inter <sup class="source-citation"><a href="https://www.romatoday.it/sport/basket/bc-roma-doncic-partnership-as-roma.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="Source 7">[7]</a></sup>.

But let us go back to June 26.

Besides presenting the team, Dončić also introduces his first major signing: [**Nico Mannion**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_Mannion).

A naturalised Italian point guard, Mannion has played, in order, for the Golden State Warriors, Virtus Bologna, Baskonia, Varese and Olimpia Milano, also becoming one of the leading figures of the Italian national team.

Ever since his college days with the Arizona Wildcats, he has carried the nickname **“Red Mamba”** <sup class="source-citation"><a href="https://www.corriere.it/sport/19_dicembre_07/mannion-red-bamba-che-ha-scelto-l-italia-l-azzurro-mi-portera-nba-9528affc-1832-11ea-addc-85aa5b33ebd7.shtml" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="Source 8">[8]</a></sup>.

And Nico Mannion is exactly who I will use to explain what I have to do with this entire story.

## March 12, 2025

We need to take a step back.

I, my Bachelor's thesis supervisor [**Marco Sciandrone**](https://www.linkedin.com/in/marco-sciandrone-1777a4234/), and [**Valerio Bianchini**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerio_Bianchini), practically a piece of Italian and Roman basketball history, find ourselves having lunch together.

Yes, it sounds like the beginning of a joke.

Knowing about my passion for basketball, the professor had invited me to a team-building lecture given by Bianchini at the Faculty of Management Engineering <sup class="source-citation"><a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/michele-magrini_today-thanks-to-an-invitation-from-professor-activity-7305690282560294912-aiAy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="Source 9">[9]</a></sup>.

The lecture is followed by the most random of lunches.

And, as chance would have it, one of the players discussed that day is none other than Nico Mannion.

About whom “Il Vate” was not exactly enthusiastic.

The curious thing is that today Mannion and Bianchini are both part of the BC Roma SPQR project.

And it is precisely thanks to the relationship between the professor and the former coach that, some time later, we find ourselves on an exploratory call with [**Roberto Carmenati**](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Carmenati), himself a former coach and Dallas Mavericks scout.

Carmenati would later arrange our meeting with the SPQR coaching staff.

## September 1, 2026

And so we arrive at September 1.

A date that, symbolically at least, marks the end of summer for a university student: the moment when you start thinking seriously again about exam season and the exams you left behind before the holidays.

For me, everything was particularly concentrated into that one day.

At **1:00 p.m.**, I had my oral Data Mining exam.

At **3:00 p.m.**, I had the long-awaited meeting with the coaching staff of Rome's new team.

The exam goes well.

The professor picks me up and we set off for the team's state-of-the-art training centre.

We enter the meeting room, from which we can glimpse the beautiful basketball court.

After a few minutes' delay, the members of the coaching staff arrive one by one:

* [**Alessandro Magro**](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Magro), Head Coach, winner of the Serie A Coach of the Year award in the 2020–21 season with Brescia;
* [**Edoardo Casalone**](https://www.linkedin.com/in/edoardocasalone/?locale=en), Defensive Coordinator and former assistant to Pozzecco on the national team, the one who often ended up in the spotlight after Poz was ejected;
* [**Stefan Ivanović**](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Ivanovi%C4%87), Offensive Coordinator, former player and son of the great [Duško Ivanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1ko_Ivanovi%C4%87);
* [**Davide Bonora**](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Bonora), Individual Improvement, a former point guard who also played in Rome and was nicknamed “Pandorino.”

The professor introduces our role: we are there as “Artificial Intelligence experts.”

More importantly, he immediately makes it clear that this is not meant to be a formal meeting.

The goal is to talk, understand their needs and identify the concrete problems we might try to tackle together.

The coaches are immediately engaged.

Alessandro Magro, in particular, launches into a fascinating stream of consciousness about basketball, data, the staff's daily work and all the problems that technology might perhaps help solve.

Stefan is very engaged too.

Until Edoardo, at one point, jokingly blurts out:

**“Hey, if we don't talk about defence too, I'm leaving!”**

Davide, on the other hand, strikes me as a little more traditional and, perhaps for that very reason, highly attentive and critical, in a positive sense, of the opportunities we were presenting to them.

At the end of the meeting, I find myself completely agreeing with what the professor would later say:

**“We got along wonderfully.”** <sup class="source-citation"><a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/marco-sciandrone-1777a4234_basket-club-roma-spqr-%C3%A8-un-nuovo-club-di-activity-7500610752186228736-sPXw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" aria-label="Source 10">[10]</a></sup>

Once the meeting is over, we move towards the court for the traditional group photo.

There is a ball on the hardwood, slowly rolling towards me.

I pick it up.

It comes naturally.

I stand well beyond the three-point line and shoot.

As soon as the ball leaves my hand, I think:

**it's perfect.**

The ball goes in without so much as moving the net.

I turn around.

They had all seen it.

Naturally, they tell me to do it again.

And this is where it gets interesting.

I move a little farther back.

Second shot.

In.

At that point, someone inevitably brings out one of the great classics of Italian culture:

**“Good things come in threes.”**

I pick up the ball again.

I stand even farther away.

I shoot.

Miss.

Thankfully.

Because otherwise the title of this article would probably have been:

**“How I Ended Up in Serie A Thanks to AI.”**

Unbelievable.

We then head towards the exit with the promise that we will speak again in the following days and begin developing an initial “pilot” idea, something concrete we can build together.

And it is precisely as we are leaving that the final coincidence in this story occurs.

Through the door walks one of the people with whom this entire story began:

**Nico Mannion.**

He had just returned from the national team.

And so, almost closing a circle that had begun a year and a half earlier at that lunch with Bianchini, I even manage to get a photo with him.

**P.S.**: That is how the story began, but there will be updates on how it evolves and on the research work throughout the entire sporting season.
